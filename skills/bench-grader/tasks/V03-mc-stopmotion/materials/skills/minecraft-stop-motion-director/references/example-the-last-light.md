# Worked Example: The Last Light

Use this case study only to understand how the method translates into a concrete one-minute film. Do not mechanically copy the story.

## Theme and emotional engine

**Premise:** Steve crosses a snowstorm and finds an abandoned cabin.

**Core emotion:** isolation transforming into warmth and companionship.

**Hero prop:** lantern.

The lantern operates as a Minecraft-world object, a practical cinematic light, a plot goal, and an emotional symbol. The environment moves from cold blue darkness to warm amber shelter.

## Eight-beat structure

1. Snowfield and abandoned cabin establish cold isolation.
2. Steve enters the environment.
3. A wolf and the damaged/unlit shelter create a reason to stay.
4. Steve chooses to repair the cabin.
5. He gathers wood and works with blocks/tools.
6. He crafts/places/activates the lantern.
7. Warm light changes the relationship between Steve, wolf, and cabin.
8. Camera pulls away from the glowing shelter into the snow.

## Camera logic

Use separately pre-rigged cameras for:

- opening wide environmental shot;
- Steve/wolf interaction;
- axe action;
- construction/crafting detail;
- lantern activation close-up;
- paired emotional composition;
- final wide pull-back.

The camera is not a game spectator. Each shot has one narrative job.

## Stop-motion logic

Render output at 24 fps while changing character poses at 12 fps. Let snow, fog, and camera motion remain smoother. Emphasize contact frames for axe strikes, block placement, and lantern handling.

## Lighting logic

Exterior: cool blue/teal snow ambience.
Interior/lantern: amber/gold practical light.

The climax is not merely "the lantern appears"; the surrounding blocks and characters visibly inherit the new warm light.

## Sound logic

Opening: wind and sparse tonal bed.
Action: snow steps, axe impacts, block interactions, workbench/crafting sounds.
Climax: lantern ignition/placement cue plus music bloom.
Ending: music resolves while wind remains outside the shelter.

## QC lessons

Before full render, inspect keyframes for:

- cabin walls occluding Steve;
- axe grip/contact;
- lantern actually attached to its support;
- wolf feet on terrain;
- warm light affecting the cabin after activation;
- snow excluded from sealed interior space.

The production principle is transferable: build the emotional arc and the physical interaction logic before spending time on the full render.
