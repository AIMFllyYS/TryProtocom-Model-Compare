## 1. Core model

Treat Minecraft as a film language rather than a game session. Convert a user theme through this chain:

**Theme -> emotion -> dramatic question -> simple story -> Minecraft-native actions -> visual motif -> shot progression -> ending echo.**

The best short usually contains one protagonist, one main objective, one obstacle/change, one strong visual motif, and one clear ending state. Complexity should appear in staging, atmosphere, and craft rather than in plot density.

## 2. Theme to emotion

Before building a set, choose the intended emotional residue. The same theme can support different films:

- First trip to The End -> awe/heroism
- First trip to The End -> paranoia/horror
- First trip to The End -> grief/sacrifice
- First trip to The End -> wonder/friendship

Force an explicit sentence: **"At the end, the viewer should feel __ because __ changed."**

Use this to reject attractive but irrelevant shots.

## 3. One-minute story compression

A one-minute film cannot support multiple independent arcs. Prefer:

- one location or a tightly linked pair of locations;
- one main character, optionally one companion or threat;
- one goal stated visually;
- one escalation;
- one decisive action;
- one afterimage.

A reliable dramatic sentence is:

**[Character] wants [concrete goal], but [obstacle], so [Minecraft-native action], causing [visible change], leaving [emotional result].**

Example:

Steve wants shelter in a snowstorm, but finds only an abandoned cabin; after gathering wood and crafting a lantern, he restores the cabin and shares its warmth with a wolf.

## 4. The eight-beat structure

For roughly 60 seconds, default to eight beats. Adjust timings to the requested duration rather than mechanically forcing these numbers.

1. **World:** establish scale, weather, danger, or beauty.
2. **Character:** reveal who is here and how they feel.
3. **Problem:** show the object, threat, absence, or opportunity.
4. **Decision:** make the protagonist choose an action.
5. **Work/escalation:** show effort, cost, or growing danger.
6. **Turn:** deliver the strongest action or reveal.
7. **Result:** show the new relationship/state of the world.
8. **Echo:** end on a visual motif that now has new meaning.

The last image should rhyme with the first whenever possible: dark to lit, empty to inhabited, closed portal to open portal, intact bridge to broken bridge, lone figure to pair, silence to distant threat.

## 5. Minecraft-ification

Do not merely replace a human actor with Steve. Rewrite the action so the world mechanics matter.

Generic action -> Minecraft-native action examples:

- repair a home -> chop logs, place planks, replace blocks, craft a light
- cross a chasm -> bridge with blocks, water bucket, mine a route
- solve a lock -> redstone circuit, lever, pressure plate, key-item ritual
- find medicine -> potion brewing, golden apple, rare ingredient quest
- escape a monster -> block placement, terrain use, shield, bow, minecart
- open another world -> portal construction/activation

Treat crafting and block placement as dramatic verbs. They should change the scene and advance the story.

## 6. Hero props and visual metaphors

Choose one prop with at least three functions:

1. gameplay/world logic;
2. clear visual focus;
3. narrative/emotional meaning.

Strong examples: lantern, diamond, sword, eye of ender, totem, music disc, flower, compass, redstone torch, map, chest key, flint and steel.

Introduce the prop early enough that the climax feels earned. Change its state or context by the end.

## 7. Story readability without dialogue

Assume the film must work muted and without subtitles. Use:

- clear sightlines;
- reaction poses;
- motivated eyelines;
- before/after environmental changes;
- inserts of key props;
- simple geography;
- pauses after reveals;
- repeated motifs.

Avoid plot points that exist only in narration unless the user explicitly wants voiceover.

## 8. Theme examples

### Ancient City horror

Emotion: dread.
Hero prop: one torch.
Arc: confident descent -> sound-triggered fear -> torch extinguishes -> darkness answers.
Ending echo: same corridor, now with a distant silhouette.

### Nether rescue

Emotion: urgency turning to relief.
Hero prop: fire-resistance potion.
Arc: portal arrival -> trapped companion -> blaze pressure -> risky bridge -> potion handoff -> escape.
Ending echo: portal closes as lava light fades.

### Cherry grove friendship

Emotion: quiet tenderness.
Hero prop: sapling/flower.
Arc: solitary builder -> injured allay/animal -> shelter -> shared planting -> blossom reveal.
Ending echo: wide shot of two figures under the new tree.
